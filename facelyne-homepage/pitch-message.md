## Email

**Subject:** A homepage concept for Facelyne (walkthrough video inside)

Hi [Name],

I've been looking closely at facelyne.io. The product is strong. Five photos, five looks and a 12-week plan is a clear, memorable promise, and the honesty in your FAQ is rare in this category.

The website doesn't do the product justice yet, so I designed a new homepage concept to show what it could be. A 90-second walkthrough is attached.

**What's holding the current site back**

1. **A contradiction that costs trust.** The FAQ says Facelyne doesn't assign attractiveness scores, but the hero mock-up shows "7.4/10" and "Jawline 7.9". A careful visitor notices that.
2. **Women don't see themselves.** The site says "for men and women", but both before/after stories are men ("Same guys.").
3. **Your biggest advantage is invisible.** "A facial specialist reviews your scan by hand" is what separates you from free AI apps, but visitors never meet that person.
4. **The research leans on insecurity.** The headline stat is about callbacks for attractive *men*. It sets an anxious tone, and it doesn't speak to half your audience.
5. **The main button doesn't say what happens.** "Start my analysis" appears six times without telling people what they're starting or how long it takes. Menu labels like "The possibilities" don't say where they go.
6. **Real and AI images look the same.** Customer photos and AI previews sit side by side with similar styling, so it's hard to know what's real.
7. **Price terms feel hidden.** "Review payment terms before confirming in checkout" makes people wonder what the catch is.
8. **It looks like many other wellness sites.** Navy, bronze and cream with tidy cards. Nothing on the page feels like hair, mirrors or a stylist's chair.

**What the new concept fixes**

- **A clear idea: "Tailored, not transformed."** The design is built around a stylist's contact sheet: your five guided photos as film frames, with notes marked up by hand. The method becomes the visual identity.
- **No scores, anywhere.** Reports read like stylist notes ("lift the crown, keep weight off the temples"), which matches your FAQ.
- **Equal proof for women and men,** with clear "Real" and "Preview" labels on every image.
- **Named specialists** with experience and a line in their own words, plus a simple three-step review process. The human review becomes the headline.
- **Buttons that win the squint test.** One action colour, used only for buttons. One label everywhere: "Take my 5 photos · 3 min". A button at every point where people decide. On mobile, it stays docked within thumb reach.
- **Interactive moments that sell the product:**
  - Tap through the five photo angles and see the specialist's note change.
  - Switch between five looks for one customer and see why each works, the daily effort and the upkeep.
  - Drag a before/after slider.
  - Enter an event date ("wedding on 14 March") and the 12-week plan works back from it.
  - Try a camera-guide demo that shows how easy the five photos are.
- **Trust built in:** four plain-language privacy promises, a free-redo guarantee near the button (proposed, for you to approve), renewal terms written out, and a comparison with salon consultations and AI apps.
- **The Barber Brief:** a one-page card for each look that customers hand to their stylist. It's useful on its own, and every barber who sees one learns your name.

**What's attached**

- 90-second walkthrough video of the interactive prototype
- Full homepage design (desktop) and mobile screens
- A one-page design rationale explaining each decision

Names, quotes and figures in the concept are placeholders. Your real team and customer stories would replace them.
