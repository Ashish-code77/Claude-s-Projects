Where the site stands today

A good product in a generic wrapper.

Based on the live homepage. Keep the structure that works. Fix the parts that cost trust.

Keep and build on

  • Keep

    The 5 → 5 → 12 promise. Five photos, five looks, a 12-week plan. It is simple, easy to remember and easy to say out loud.

  • Keep

    Honest disclaimers. Studies describe averages, not guarantees. This modesty is unusual in the category. Make it part of the brand.

  • Keep

    The roadmap section. Quick wins, Build the base, Refine & reveal. Good structure. It needs better visuals.

  • Keep

    FAQ quality. Clear on privacy, medical limits and delivery (72 h, or 24 h express).

  • Keep

    Accessibility basics. Skip link, pause control on the animation, chart data available as text.

Fix first

  • Conflict

    Scores vs. "no scores". The FAQ says Facelyne does not assign attractiveness scores, but the hero mock-up shows 7.4/10 with Jawline 7.9. Pick one position and show it everywhere.

  • Proof

    "For men and women", but only men are shown. Both before/after stories are men (Same guys.). Women see no one like them.

  • Proof

    The expert has no name. A facial specialist reviews your scan by hand is the main difference from an AI app, and we never meet that person.

  • Tone

    The research leans on insecurity. 2× more job callbacks for attractive male applicants. The same study found attractive women were penalised, which undercuts the pitch to women.

  • Clarity

    Vague navigation and repeated CTAs. The approach and The possibilities don't say where they go. Start my analysis appears 6 times and never says what happens next.

  • Look

    Familiar palette. Navy, bronze and cream with tidy cards is the default wellness-SaaS look. Nothing in it says hair, mirror or chair.

Brand story

Tailored, not transformed.

A tailor doesn't change your body. They change how the jacket fits it. Facelyne does the same for your face, with haircut, brows, beard, skin routine and grooming. The site should feel like a fitting with a skilled person: someone looks closely, marks things up by hand and gives you a clear plan. It shouldn't feel like scanning yourself in a beauty app.

Marked by handGrease-pencil notes, circled frames and stylist arrows show that a person was involved, without having to say it.
Shown, not scoredDrop the 10-point numbers. Use stylist language: "frames the jaw", "lifts the crown", "softens the forehead".
Calm and kindNo glow-up fear. Small changes, your choice, said plainly. That tone already exists in the FAQ, so carry it into the headlines.
The visitor's story

One visit to the new site, from first screen to sign-up.

Meet Rhea, 31, who has had the same haircut since college and has a wedding in four months. Each step is something the new site has to do.

0 s · The scroll

A contact sheet stops her thumb.

Rhea finds Facelyne through an Instagram reel called "One face, five looks". The landing page opens on five frames of a real woman's face, one circled in red pencil. No stock smiles. No 10-point score.

Hero job: show the method in one image.

10 s · The promise

"Five photos. Five looks. One you'll keep."

One plain line explains the product. Below it: "Results in 72 hours · Reviewed by a named specialist · No surgery, no filters." The button says Take my 5 photos (3 min), so she knows exactly what clicking does.

40 s · The proof

She sees someone like her.

A swipeable lookbook: a woman with a similar face shape and her five looks laid out like magazine pages. One look is marked "her pick", with a short quote from week 12. Real photos and AI previews have clearly different labels.

1 min · The person

She meets the specialist.

"Hi, I'm Nadia. I've been cutting hair for 11 years, and I'll review your photos." A face, a name, credentials and a hand-written note on a sample report. This is what makes Facelyne different from a free AI app.

Trust comes from a real person, not a statistic.

2 min · The plan

She can picture her 12 weeks.

The roadmap appears as a wall calendar with her wedding date pinned at week 16. She opens a sample "Barber Brief" card, the one-page sheet she would hand to her stylist, and decides it is worth $69.99 for that alone.

3 min · The yes

Clear price, no surprises.

"$69.99 for 12 weeks. About 83¢ a day." Renewal terms sit in plain text beside the button, not hidden behind "review payment terms". She starts the camera guide.

Week 12 · The re-scan

The before and after becomes something to share.

Her re-scan produces a two-frame contact sheet she can post. Facelyne gets a new story for the lookbook, and Rhea gets a referral code to share.

Photography rules
  • Real customers, the same daylight and the same five angles. The guide's consistency becomes the visual style.
  • Half women, half men, a range of ages, skin tones and hair textures.
  • Hands at work: a stylist's comb, a brow pencil, a phone on a stand.
  • Light film grain and no retouching beyond exposure. Never use stock.
  • AI previews always carry a small "Preview" stamp. Real results don't.

New homepage, scene by scene

Eight scenes, in scroll order.

The current sections are mostly the right ones. The order changes so that proof and the specialist come before the research, and pricing comes right after the plan.

1

Contact-sheet hero

A real customer's five frames, one circled. Headline, one line of copy, and a CTA that says what happens next.

2

The lookbook

Swipeable stories, half women and half men. Five looks per person, with "their pick" marked. Clear labels for real photos and AI previews.

3

Meet the specialists

Faces, names, years of experience. A short clip of a review being marked up by hand.

4

How it works

Three steps: 3 minutes, 72 hours, 12 weeks. Shown as the camera guide, a sample report, then the pick.

5

Inside the report

A real, annotated sample report page and the Barber Brief card. Remove the 10-point scores.

6

The 12 weeks

The tailor's-tape timeline. Visitors can enter an event date ("wedding in 16 weeks") and the plan adjusts.

7

Price, plainly

$69.99 for 12 weeks, renewal terms in plain words, and the express 24 h option shown here instead of only at checkout.

8

Research, FAQ, close

Research moves lower, framed around what you can change. FAQ stays. The close: Your face isn't generic.

Creative ideas

Features people will share.

Lead idea

The Barber Brief

A one-page card in every report that the customer shows their stylist: clipper guard numbers, length at the crown, neckline, product, and a sketch. It solves a real problem ("I never know what to ask for"). It also works as a free lead magnet with a generic version per face shape, and every barber who sees one learns about Facelyne.

Interactive

Mirror mode

On the "See it before you start" slider, let visitors flip between the five looks on one demo face, with specialist notes drawing on for each one. It replaces the current two-image drag comparison.

Content

"One face, five looks" series

A weekly reel and a matching blog post: one volunteer, five directions, the audience votes, and the reveal comes at week 12. It gives the brand ongoing stories to tell.

SEO

Face-shape library

Pages like "haircuts for a round face" and "beard styles for a long face", with real contact sheets. Right now the site has one indexable page, and this is where search traffic comes from.

Occasion

Countdown plans

"Wedding in 16 weeks." "New job in 8." Visitors enter a date and the 12-week plan works back from it. Good for landing pages and for gifting.

Growth

Gift a fitting

A printable gift card designed as a contact sheet with empty frames. Suits birthdays, grooms' parties and Father's Day.

Web-dev checklist

Under the hood.

The site runs on Next.js, which handles all of this well. Most items take a day or less.

Image pipeline for the new photography

next/image with AVIF/WebP, real width and height on every image, and the hero frame preloaded as the LCP image. Target LCP under 2.0 s on mid-range Android.

Structured data

FAQPage, Product/Offer (price, currency, 3-month term) and Organization schema. The FAQ content is ready for this already.

Open Graph images that sell

Generated OG cards in the contact-sheet style for the homepage, each face-shape page and shared re-scans.

Funnel analytics

Track hero CTA, lookbook swipe, sample report open, pricing view, checkout start, and each photo step (1–5) so you can see where people drop off.

A/B test the hero

Test the current headline against "Five photos. Five looks." and the CTA wording. Run each for two weeks.

Motion with restraint

One drawing animation for the pencil marks, using SVG stroke-dashoffset and no heavy libraries. Respect reduced-motion settings, as the current pause control already does.

Mobile-first camera guide

Most visitors from reels arrive on phones. Make the 5-photo guide work in one hand without zooming, before any visual polish.

Content routes

/looks, /specialists, /guides/[face-shape], /gift. Statically generated, with their own sitemap entries.
